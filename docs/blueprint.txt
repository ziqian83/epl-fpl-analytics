FPL ANALYTICS PLAYGROUND - PROJECT BLUEPRINT
Prepared 11 Sep 2026 for Ziqian. Working document: the "what and why", so you can
direct the build without touching the technical plumbing.


1. THE IDEA

One dataset, two payoffs. First, a showcase: ten seasons of English Premier
League data sitting in ClickHouse Cloud, queried in plain English through the
new ChatGPT data connector - a live, personal demo of the "ChatGPT as front
door to the data warehouse" story you tell boards. Second, an edge: the same
tables power a backtested player-performance model and a Fantasy Premier
League picker that spots undervalued players before the crowd does.


2. ARCHITECTURE (zero code)

Three pieces, no programming anywhere:

  a. ClickHouse Cloud SQL console - the engine. All data loading and every
     model below runs as plain SQL pasted into the browser console. The two
     public data sources are loaded directly from their URLs using
     ClickHouse's built-in url() table function: no downloads, no scripts,
     no servers to babysit.
  b. Public CSV sources (free, no sign-up):
     - vaastav/Fantasy-Premier-League on GitHub: official FPL data per
       player per gameweek, 2016-17 through 2025-26.
     - football-data.co.uk: match-level results and stats (E0.csv),
       same 10 seasons.
  c. ChatGPT data connector - the natural-language layer. Once the tables
     exist, the ClickHouse plugin lets ChatGPT write the SQL for you, live
     against the service. Questions in, answers out, nothing exported.

The load script is already written and tested end-to-end against a local
ClickHouse (253,900 player-gameweek rows, 3,800 matches, 7,358 players,
all 10 seasons complete). It goes in the moment the Cloud trial is
reactivated.


3. DATA MODEL

Three raw tables plus one derived table. Column lists below are verified
against the real CSV headers, season by season.

  epl_fpl_gameweeks  (one row per player per gameweek, ~254k rows)
    season, gw, name, position, team, player_id, opponent_team_id,
    was_home, kickoff_time, round, minutes, goals_scored, assists,
    expected_goals, expected_assists, expected_goal_involvements, starts,
    selected, value (FPL price x10), total_points, team_h_score,
    team_a_score
    Caveats found in the files: position/team exist from 2020-21 onward;
    expected-goals fields and starts exist from 2022-23 onward (empty
    before that); player_id is reissued every season, so cross-season
    matching uses player_code (see below).

  epl_fpl_players_raw  (one row per player per season, FPL's own season
  aggregates, ~7.4k rows)
    season, player_id, player_code (stable across seasons - the join key),
    web_name, first_name, second_name, position_id, team_id, now_cost
    (price x10), selected_by_percent (ownership %), total_points, minutes,
    goals_scored, assists, expected_goals, expected_assists (2022-23+),
    starts (2022-23+)

  epl_matches  (one row per match, 380 per season, 3,800 total)
    season, match_date, match_time, home_team, away_team,
    full-time and half-time goals and result (fthg, ftag, ftr, hthg,
    htag, htr), referee, shots, shots on target, corners, yellows, reds
    for each side. The source files also carry bookmaker odds columns;
    not loaded yet, easy to add if you ever want a betting-angle demo.

  epl_player_season  (derived; the backtest workhorse)
    One row per player per season: season, player_id, player_code, name,
    position, team (last club that season), minutes, starts, goals,
    assists, xg, xa, fpl_points_from_gws, fpl_price_x10,
    fpl_selected_by_percent, fpl_total_points.


4. BACKTEST MODULE (train 2021-22..2024-25, test 2025-26)

  Cohort rule: players with 900+ minutes in both the training window and
  the test season (a full season is ~3,420; 900 keeps regulars and drops
  cameos). 203 players qualified for the 2023-24 to 2024-25 dry run.

  Baseline to beat: "next season = last season". Measured on real data,
  last season's FPL points correlate with next season's at 0.52 (Pearson,
  2023-24 vs 2024-25). That is the number any model must beat. (Final
  metric: rank correlation, which is fairer to outliers - the plumbing
  supports both.)

  Model candidates, in order of effort:
    i.  Weighted 3-season form: 0.5 x last season + 0.3 x season before
        + 0.2 x the one before that, per 90 minutes, times expected
        minutes.
    ii. Minutes trend: reward players whose minutes are rising season
        over season (new starters), penalise decliners.
    iii.Age curve: peak years roughly 24-29 for outfield players. Needs
        dates of birth, which the FPL files do not carry - optional
        add-on from a third source, only if i and ii leave us short.

  Verdict rule: a model ships only if it beats 0.52 on the test season.
  If nothing beats the naive baseline, that is itself a publishable
  finding ("FPL points are barely predictable year to year").


5. FPL PICKER MODULE

  Per upcoming gameweek, per player:
    predicted points = recent form (per-90 FPL points, weighted last
      5-10 gameweeks) x expected minutes factor x fixture difficulty
      (opponent's goals conceded per match from epl_matches).
    differential score = predicted points vs selected_by_percent.
      High prediction + low ownership = the pick that wins mini-leagues.
  The 2025-26 dry run already surfaces the right shape: defenders like
  Guéhi and Senesi top the points-per-million list, and a <5% ownership
  filter finds the Casemiro-type differentials.


6. SHOWCASE LAYER

  Demo queries that have already been run against the loaded data:
    - Top scorer every season for 10 years, one query.
      (Kane 29 in 2016-17 ... Haaland 36 in 2022-23 ... Salah 29 in 24-25.)
    - "Which players beat their expected goals two years running?"
      (Phil Foden +20.2 goals over xG across three seasons leads.)
    - Best FPL value this season: points per million spent, 900+ minutes.
    - Differentials: highest scorers owned by under 5% of managers.
    - Home advantage by season: 49% home wins in 2016-17 down to 38% in
      the behind-closed-doors 2020-21 season - a one-row COVID story.
    - "Is last year's form a good predictor?" - the 0.52 baseline itself,
      computed live.
  Every one of these is a natural-language question the ChatGPT connector
  can answer live in a board demo. That is the operating-model story:
  the warehouse stays put, the questions come to it.

  The board translation table - each picker signal is a familiar piece of
  corporate data work wearing a football kit:
    consistency (4+ pts when 60+ min)  -> revenue quality: repeatable,
                                          dependable quarters, not one-off
                                          spikes
    team-proof (W/D/L splits)          -> alpha vs beta: does the unit
                                          outperform, or just ride a rising
                                          market
    form-sustain (streak lengths)      -> initiative decay: how long a good
                                          run typically lasts, and when to
                                          intervene
    fixture weighting (opp. strength)  -> territory-adjusted performance
                                          measurement: fair comparisons once
                                          difficulty is priced in
    points per million                 -> ROI discipline under a budget cap
    minutes security (starts %, mins)  -> key-person risk: is the output
                                          dependent on someone always on
                                          the pitch
    transfer economics (-4 hit rule)   -> cost of switching: act only when
                                          the expected gain over the hold
                                          horizon beats the switching cost
  The demo line writes itself: "the same seven questions, asked of your
  sales pipeline instead of my fantasy team."

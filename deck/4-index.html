The business problem

History is abundant. Priced decisions are not.

  • Resource allocation under constraint. Every week: a fixed budget, a scarce number of moves, and a switching cost for each one. That is capital allocation, procurement, and headcount planning in miniature.
  • Signal vs story. Reputations, streaks and narratives all claim to be information. Almost none survive contact with ten years of records.
  • Timing under uncertainty. When to act, when to hold, and what the right to act later is worth - an options question, not a hunch.

The test bed: 253,900 player performances across ten completed seasons of the English Premier League, in a ClickHouse Cloud warehouse, queried live. Football is the proof. The method is the product.

The honest baseline

First, measure what doing nothing smart is worth.

0.517

Correlation of "next season = last season", 203 regulars, 2023-24 to 2024-25. Any model that cannot beat the naive forecast does not ship.

0.454

The same on rank correlation - the final metric, fairer to outliers. The bar is published before the models run, not fitted after.

No single signal cleared this bar on its own. That is not a failure of the project - it is the finding that disciplines it.

What survived the backtest

Three signals earn their place.

Consistency

Share of full matches returning 4+ points. Top quintile went on to beat the cohort average 73.5% of the time vs a 46.7% base rate. The squad screen: dependable separates from streaky.

Fixture weighting

Opponent defensive strength, four tiers, ~13,800 appearances each: 4.04 / 3.75 / 3.37 / 2.98 points per appearance. Monotonic. The weakest tier is worth +36%.

The cold-start prior

For players with no history, the market's opening price predicts the season at 0.534 - matching the veteran baseline. Trust the price for attackers fast; discount new defenders until ~900 minutes.

With caveats: value-per-million screens the cheap end (59.2% top-quintile precision); team-proofing is real but inverted from the pub story - players whose sides lose when they play score less next season, not more.

What did not survive

The busts are the credibility.

0.001

Season-to-season persistence of home/away scoring splits, 173 regulars. A favourite pub stat, dead in the data. Retired - kept only as same-season colour.

0.083

Historical availability as a predictor of future output. Rotation and injury risk are live information, not history. Moved to the news layer.

Every retired signal is logged with the query that killed it. An operating model that cannot say "we were wrong, here is the number" is a marketing deck.

Data quality is the moat

The errors are documented because they were made.

  • Join discipline. The platform reissues player IDs every season. Join across seasons on the wrong key and the model scores -0.07 where the right key scores 0.52. Different systems disagreeing on identity: a common problem in corporates.
  • Cold starts, three variants. No history, thin-and-stale history, full-but-dated history - each gets a different prior, and live evidence overrules fast for young players on a development curve.
  • Entity resolution. Three name formats across ten years of files; the stable cross-season key carries every join.
  • Query scoping. A "missing" player was a one-season query over a ten-season problem. Scope is a design decision, not a default.

The engine, live

Run on a real squad, this week.

  • Best value held: a 4.8m defender at 4.38 points-per-million with 0.667 consistency - the backtested archetype.
  • The flag: the squad's most expensive midfielder (12.0m) reads 0.333 consistency and 2.25 ppm behind one 23-point home haul. The model says this is the profile to sell before the crowd, not after.
  • The watchlist: three clean sheets from a newly promoted side - and the cold-start rule says discount new defenders. Watchlist, not buy.
  • The discipline: two banked transfers, no move that fails to clear the 4-point toll. Patience is also a position.
